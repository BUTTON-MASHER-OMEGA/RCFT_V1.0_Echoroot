# D1 — Two-Lane Architecture

**Decision: Preserve mandatory two-lane architecture in RCMT; retain optionality in RCF.**

RCF intentionally relaxed the original mandatory memory/motion two-lane requirement to an optional architectural profile.

RCMT independently adopts a mandatory two-lane architecture consisting of:

- A symplectic mode-space lane.
- A geometric lane.

This is a valid downstream specialization and does not conflict with RCF's optional profile.

### Authorized changes

- Preserve the mandatory two-lane requirement in RCMT.
- Preserve the optional two-lane profile in RCF.
- Clarify that RCMT's requirement is an adopted architectural constraint.
- Record the historical memory/motion rationale where supported.
- Distinguish architectural adoption from a demonstrated mathematical necessity theorem.
- Remove language implying that RCF universally requires the same architecture.

### Restrictions

Do not demote RCMT's two-lane requirement.

Do not invent a necessity proof or assert that projection alone establishes the requirement.

# D3 — Physics Interface Responsibilities

**Decision: Preserve both interface families with distinct responsibilities.**

The apparent conflict between the physics-interface README and the pipeline Physics Interface Contract arises from insufficiently distinguished consumer stages.

The intended separation is:

**Construction-facing interfaces**

May consume explicitly authorized local and upstream records to construct projected representations.

**Effective-output physics consumers**

Consume exported projected artifacts and must not independently access raw substrate structures.

### Authorized changes

- Document the two interface responsibilities explicitly.
- Clarify which permissions apply at each stage.
- Preserve the pipeline contract's restrictions for effective-output consumers.
- Preserve authorized local-record access for construction-facing adapters.
- Identify ambiguous modules requiring explicit stage assignment.

### Restrictions

Do not merge the two permission sets into one unrestricted interface.

Do not assume that directory placement establishes consumer permissions.

Do not introduce new APIs or module ownership decisions without authorization.

# D7 — Projection Down and Relational Carriers

**Decision: Treat Π↓ as a downstream compatibility diagnostic, not an inverse reconstruction mechanism.**

Projection responsibilities must remain separated from upstream survivor selection.

The intended interpretation is:

- **Π↑:** Constructs a representation from authorized discrete survivor records.
- **Π↓:** Evaluates downstream compatibility, consistency, or representational loss.

Π↓ does not independently reconstruct substrate state or control upstream survivorship.

### Authorized changes

- Clarify the respective domains and responsibilities of Π↑ and Π↓.
- Describe Π↓ as a diagnostic or compatibility operation.
- Remove or qualify unsupported reconstruction and fixed-point claims.
- Preserve the geometry-neutral LCO contract.
- Restrict triangle-specific evaluation to explicitly declared downstream profiles.
- Clarify that global-invariant evaluation may require adjacency together with declared orientation or transport information.

### Restrictions

Do not introduce an inverse-projection algorithm.

Do not authorize feedback into the upstream survivor sieve.

Do not introduce new triangle-packing requirements into the core LCO.

Do not invent missing carrier structures or transport algorithms.

# D8 — LCO Evaluation and Update Policy

**Decision: The LCO is an evaluator, not a monotonic optimization mechanism.**

The LCO evaluates supplied admissible candidates and returns the highest-coherence or lowest-mismatch result according to its scoring contract.

It makes **no guarantee that neighborhood mismatch decreases** relative to the previous state.

Lowest mismatch means lowest among the evaluated candidates, not necessarily lower than the neighborhood's previous mismatch.

### Authorized changes

1. Remove universal monotonic-descent claims attributed to the LCO.
2. Clarify that the LCO ranks or evaluates candidates under its declared scoring contract.
3. Clarify that selecting the best available candidate does not guarantee improvement over the previous neighborhood state.
4. Preserve the separation between evaluation, update-policy decisions, and state mutation.
5. Distinguish deterministic scoring from stochastic selection policies.
6. Where separately declared policy profiles impose acceptance restrictions, document those restrictions as policy-specific rather than LCO guarantees.
7. Correct examples that imply the LCO guarantees convergence or decreasing mismatch.

### Restrictions

The LCO does not independently mutate neighborhood state.

The LCO does not guarantee convergence, zero mismatch, or basin stabilization.

Do not implement or select a new update algorithm.

Do not impose a universal descent requirement.

**This decision explicitly replaces the audit's recommendation to preserve non-increasing mismatch as a general LCO commitment.**

# D9 — GCM Indexing, Routing, and Historical 12/36-Mode Construction

**Decision: Preserve historical indexing and clarify its target-driven origin.**

The historical mode construction used:

- **12 antipodal-pair modes.**
- **3 offset regimes.**
- **36 indexed configurations** from the 12 × 3 construction.

The three offset regimes appear to have been motivated by the target of representing the Standard Model's three generations.

They should not be presented as an independently derived prediction of three generations.

The precise historical offset interpretation remains subject to source verification.

### Authorized changes

- Confirm and correct the authoritative public GCM indexing reference.
- Preserve the original 12/36-mode construction and its historical rationale.
- Distinguish geometric indexing from physical interpretation.
- Describe the three-regime choice as historically target-driven unless independent derivation evidence is recovered.
- Preserve independently established geometric properties of the antipodal-pair construction.
- Keep proposed 24-cell routing identities provisional unless explicitly adopted.
- Preserve SMG-specific mode policies as downstream model commitments where already adopted.

### Restrictions

Do not promote 12 or 36 modes into universal GCM requirements.

Do not describe three generations as independently derived from the geometry.

Do not silently remove existing SMG adopter-specific policies; classify their scope and evidential status correctly.

Do not invent a new generation mechanism or routing architecture.

# D10 — RCP-1.2 Protocol Clarification and Full RCMT Migration

**Decision: Clarify the RCP-1.2 header specification and upgrade the entire RCMT Markdown documentation corpus to RCP-1.2.**

RCF was previously migrated to RCP-1.2.

RCMT was not comprehensively migrated.

The owner now explicitly authorizes a repository-wide RCMT migration.

## A. Header sentinel clarification

RCP-1.2 contains ambiguous wording concerning a leading header sentinel.

Clarify that the required opening structure is:

1. A single document H1 heading.
2. The canonical Document Contract immediately following the heading.

Do not require an additional undefined delimiter or sentinel marker.

Update corresponding validation language.

## B. RCMT migration scope

Upgrade all RCMT Markdown documents that are not already compliant with RCP-1.2.

This includes applicable:

- Local and global documentation.
- Constraints and invariants.
- Physics interfaces.
- SMG documentation.
- Module documentation.
- READMEs and indexes.
- Other Markdown files within the RCMT repository.

## C. Migration requirements

- Use `rcf/rcp-1.2.md` as the authoritative protocol.
- Apply the required header structure, metadata fields, field naming, and ordering.
- Preserve scientific and mathematical content.
- Preserve existing ownership, authority, scope, status, and dependency relationships.
- Verify the meaning of legacy `depends_on` fields before mapping them to canonical upstream dependencies.
- Do not invent missing dependencies or authority assignments.
- Preserve the historical status of older research documents.
- For frozen artifacts whose content must remain byte-for-byte unchanged, use a compliant wrapper or external manifest and record the exception.
- Validate against the full RCP-1.2 specification, not merely field presence.

## D. Execution

- Perform migration in reviewable batches.
- Keep RCF protocol corrections separate from RCMT migration changes.
- Produce a migration report identifying changed documents, exceptions, unresolved metadata, and validation results.
- Regenerate compressed artifacts after authoritative source changes are complete.
