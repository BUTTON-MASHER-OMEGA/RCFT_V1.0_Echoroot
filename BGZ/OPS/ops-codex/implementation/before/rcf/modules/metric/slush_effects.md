## purpose

This document records how upstream slush patterns are reflected when a
metric representation is applied to the continuous shadow.

Slush effects do **not** introduce new primitives. They describe how
a downstream metric, if chosen, can represent structural features that
are already present in mismatch, slush, and Π’s smoothing behavior.

---

## upstream origin

From `slush-mapping.md` and projection documentation:

- slush is the projected remainder of discrete mismatch and constraint friction,
- Π smooths but does not eliminate this residual structure,
- slush appears in the shadow as localized variation, anisotropy, and
  density‑dependent behavior in current evaluations.

This file assumes that a downstream module has chosen to fit a metric
to the continuous shadow and describes how slush‑related structure
may appear in that representation.

No physical interpretation is assigned.

---

## structural description

When a metric representation is applied to the continuous shadow:

- regions with higher slush‑related mismatch density are **observed**
  to correspond to regions where fitted distances show greater
  anisotropy in current evaluations,

- adjacency‑based irregularities upstream are **observed** to align
  with direction‑dependent variation in the fitted metric,

- density‑dependent slush patterns are **observed** to align with
  differences in how strongly the fitted metric varies across regions.

These statements are descriptive summaries of current evaluations, not
mechanisms or laws.

---

## role in metric representations

Slush effects are used to:

- motivate why effective metrics fitted on the shadow may be anisotropic
  or non‑uniform in regions associated with high slush,

- remind downstream modules that such anisotropy is a **shadow** of
  upstream mismatch and constraint friction, not an independent
  degree of freedom,

- provide structural context for interpreting metric variation as a
  representation choice over Π‑determined slush patterns.

This file does not define a metric. It documents how slush can be
represented if a metric is chosen downstream.
