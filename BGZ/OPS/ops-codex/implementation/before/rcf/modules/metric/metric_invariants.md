## purpose
This document describes the **invariants** that all metric constructions must satisfy.
These invariants ensure that the metric module remains:

- downstream‑only,
- representational,
- Π‑determined,
- coordinate‑optional,
- and non‑interpretive.

No new primitives are introduced.

---

## core invariants

### 1. downstream‑only behavior
All metric constructions are downstream of geometry‑continuous and geometry‑dynamics.
They:

- do not influence upstream modules,
- do not modify upstream structure,
- and do not introduce assumptions that propagate upward.

Metric behavior is representational only.

---

### 2. Π‑determinism
All metric constructions are fully determined by:

- adjacency structure,
- slush distribution,
- plateau organization,
- survivor‑consistent structure,
- and Π’s smoothing behavior.

No external structure contributes.

---

### 3. coordinate‑free core
All metrics are described without:

- coordinate charts,
- basis choices,
- affine connections,
- or geometric interpretation.

Any coordinate representation is downstream and optional.

---

### 4. representational neutrality
All metrics:

- do not imply curvature,
- do not imply dynamics,
- do not imply physical distance,
- and do not introduce geometric or physical meaning.

They are descriptive encodings of relational structure observed after Π.

---

### 5. locality
All metrics reflect **local** relational structure:

- adjacency contributes neighborhood proximity,
- slush contributes density‑related separation,
- plateau structure contributes γ‑consistent grouping,
- and no nonlocal operator is assumed.

Locality is descriptive, not mechanistic.

---

### 6. no new primitives
Metric constructions do not introduce:

- new objects,
- new operators,
- new structural assumptions,
- or new forms of geometry.

All representational structures are described in terms of upstream relational information.

---

### 7. compatibility across metric constructions
All metrics:

- may be used jointly in representationally,
- may be compared descriptively,
- and may be used jointly in downstream modules.

No metric construction overrides or invalidates another.

---

## role in the metric module
These invariants:

- maintain consistency across adjacency, slush, plateau, and effective metrics,
- ensure downstream safety,
- prevent upstream contamination,
- and establish compatibility with the interface contract.

They define the stable boundaries within which all metric constructions operate.
