### *Global Coherence States for Downstream Use*

Structural viability summarizes the **global coherence state** of a system based on:

- global coherence metrics,
- persistence detection results,
- cross‑scale consistency.

It produces **high‑level classifications** such as:

- coherent,
- marginal,
- unstable.

These are summaries, not control directives.

---

## 1. Inputs

Structural viability consumes:

- global coherence metrics,
- persistence indicators,
- cross‑scale consistency measures.

It does **not** consume:

- geometric data,
- temporal control parameters,
- operator‑level update rules.

---

## 2. Viability States (Conceptual)

Examples:

- **Coherent:**
  - low accumulated mismatch,
  - strong cross‑scale consistency,
  - multiple persistent low‑cost patterns.

- **Marginal:**
  - moderate mismatch,
  - partial cross‑scale tension,
  - some persistent patterns, some unstable.

- **Unstable:**
  - high mismatch accumulation,
  - strong cross‑scale conflict,
  - few or no persistent low‑cost patterns.

These are **descriptive labels** for downstream interpretation.

---

## 3. Properties

Structural viability:

- is **global**, not local,
- is **derived**, not primitive,
- is **descriptive**, not prescriptive,
- does **not** define geometry,
- does **not** define or modify time,
- does **not** perform local update selection.

---

## 4. Outputs

The module outputs:

- a viability classification (coherent / marginal / unstable),
- supporting scores and indicators,
- optional robustness indicators.

These outputs are consumed by:

- policy modules,
- control systems,
- geometry/physics layers,

which may choose to act on them, but that logic lies **outside** GCO.

---

## 5. Status

Structural viability is the final summary layer of GCO.
It compresses multiscale coherence behavior into a small set of global states for downstream use.
