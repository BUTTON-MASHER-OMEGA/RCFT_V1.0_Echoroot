### *Residual Mismatch in the Continuous Shadow*

This document defines how residual mismatch appears after projection.

Projection smooths discrete survivor structure, but it does not erase all traces of mismatch, constraint friction, or local irregularity.
The surviving remainder of that unresolved structure in the continuous shadow is referred to here as **slush**.

---

## **1. Purpose**

The purpose of this file is to prevent a common failure mode:
treating projection as though it converts a discrete survivor into a perfectly smooth and fully homogeneous continuous object.

That is not the RCF claim.

Projection smooths the discrete substrate, but persistent residual structure remains visible in the shadow.

---

## **2. What Slush Is**

Slush is the projected remainder of:

- local mismatch that persists under recursion,
- constraint friction that is not fully resolved upstream,
- anisotropic update irregularity,
- incomplete smoothing across the continuous shadow.

Slush is therefore:

- structural, not accidental,
- inherited, not introduced downstream,
- a residual of projection, not a failure of it.

---

## **3. What Projection Does to Mismatch**

Projection does not preserve raw combinatorial mismatch spikes as spikes.

Instead:

- sharp local mismatch is smoothed,
- persistent mismatch patterns are aggregated,
- unresolved irregularity appears as distributed residual structure.

At the continuous level, one therefore sees not raw discrete mismatch events, but their smoothed image as:

- gradient-like variation,
- anisotropic regions,
- curvature-like irregularity,
- ladder-sensitive residual structure.

---

## **4. Structural Role of Slush**

Slush provides the continuous shadow with a record of where discrete regularity is incomplete.

It therefore acts as an upstream source for:

- local irregularity in projected neighborhoods,
- anisotropic redistribution signatures,
- non-uniform smoothing behavior,
- later flow-sensitive variation in the continuous shadow.

This does not make slush a dynamical law.
It remains a projected structural residue.

---

## **5. Invariant Consequences**

The presence of slush means:

- projection cannot be treated as perfect homogenization,
- continuous regularity is always conditional and regime-dependent,
- downstream flow modules must act on a shadow that already contains residual structure,
- curvature-like behavior, where it later appears, should be traced first to projected residuals before being treated as an independent geometric primitive.

---

## **6. Boundary of This File**

This file does **not** define:

- the equations governing slush evolution,
- continuous flow laws,
- curvature equations,
- or the interpretation of slush in physics terms.

It defines only how residual mismatch survives into the projected shadow.

---

## **7. Status**

Slush projection defines the residual side of the projection contract.
It ensures that smoothing is not mistaken for erasure and that downstream continuous modules inherit the unresolved structure carried forward by \( \Pi \).
