### *Geometry‑Adjacent Module — Structural Specification (Draft)*

This document describes the symmetry‑alignment properties shared by all survivor geometries in the RCF sieve.
Symmetry alignment refers to the **convergence of adjacency, projection, and constraint‑density patterns** toward a consistent structural envelope.
No physical interpretation is assumed or implied.

---

## **1. Overview**

Survivor geometries exhibit a consistent pattern of symmetry alignment across γ‑plateaus and projection layers.
This alignment is not imposed; it emerges from:

- pruning pressure
- closure requirements
- projection compatibility
- constraint‑density gradients

Symmetry alignment is a structural attractor behavior of the RCF sieve.

---

## **2. Structural Meaning of Symmetry Alignment**

In this module, *symmetry* refers to:

- consistent adjacency relationships
- repeated combinatorial motifs
- stable recursive patterns
- projection‑preserved structure

Alignment means that these features converge toward a shared structural envelope across survivors.

### **2.1 Adjacency Coherence**
Survivors maintain consistent adjacency signatures across:

- local neighborhoods
- recursive subdivisions
- plateau transitions

### **2.2 Projection Coherence**
Upward and downward projections preserve:

- local degree
- combinatorial motifs
- adjacency cycles

Geometries that lose coherence under projection are pruned.

### **2.3 Constraint‑Density Coherence**
As γ increases, survivors align toward geometries with:

- higher constraint density
- lower slack
- more stable recursive patterns

This alignment is structural, not interpretive.

---

## **3. Sources of Symmetry Alignment**

Symmetry alignment arises from three independent mechanisms:

### **3.1 Pruning Pressure**
Geometries with inconsistent symmetry patterns accumulate mismatch and are eliminated.

### **3.2 Plateau Persistence**
The Chromatic 12 motif must remain stable across all γ‑plateaus.
This forces alignment between plateau‑level symmetry patterns.

### **3.3 Projection Compatibility**
Projection must preserve:

- adjacency cycles
- combinatorial motifs
- recursive structure

Only geometries with compatible symmetry patterns survive.

## **4. Symmetry Features Shared by All Survivors**

All evaluated survivors exhibit the following structural symmetry features:

### **4.1 Chromatic 12 Symmetry**
A 12‑element combinatorial motif that recurs across:

- recursive subdivisions
- projection layers
- γ‑plateaus

### **4.2 Triplicated Plateau Symmetry**
Each plateau preserves the Chromatic 12 independently, producing three aligned copies.

### **4.3 Uniform Local Degree**
Survivors maintain a consistent local degree across all vertices, supporting stable adjacency cycles.

### **4.4 Recursive Scaling Symmetry**
Adjacency relationships follow recursive scaling patterns consistent with φ‑based combinatorial structure.

### **4.5 Projection‑Stable Symmetry**
Symmetry patterns remain identifiable under both upward and downward projection.

## **5. Symmetry Alignment and the 36‑Mode Structure**

The 36‑mode structure arises from symmetry alignment:

### **5.1 Plateau‑Aligned Motifs**
Each plateau preserves the Chromatic 12 motif.

### **5.2 Cross‑Plateau Coherence**

### **5.4 Stability Under Projection**
