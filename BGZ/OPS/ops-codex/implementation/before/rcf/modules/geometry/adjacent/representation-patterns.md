### *Geometry‑Adjacent Module — Structural Specification (Draft)*

This document describes the internal representation patterns shared by all survivor geometries in the RCF sieve.
A *representation pattern* is a stable, recursively identifiable mapping between:

- combinatorial motifs
- adjacency cycles
- plateau‑aligned mode structures
- projection‑stable features

Representation patterns describe **how the geometry encodes itself**, not how it encodes physical entities.

---

## **1. Overview**

Survivor geometries exhibit consistent internal representation patterns that remain stable across:

- γ‑plateaus
- projection layers
- recursive subdivisions

These patterns arise from structural constraints, not imposed rules.
Representation patterns are emergent features of the RCF sieve.

---

## **2. Structural Meaning of Representation**

In this module, *representation* refers to:

- how combinatorial motifs recur
- how adjacency cycles encode structure
- how modes map into stable patterns
- how recursive features preserve identity

Representation is **internal** to the geometry.

### **2.1 Motif Recurrence**
Survivors reuse the same combinatorial motifs across scales and plateaus.

### **2.2 Adjacency Encoding**
Adjacency cycles encode structural relationships between modes and motifs.

### **2.3 Plateau‑Aligned Representation**
The same motif is represented consistently across all γ‑plateaus.

### **2.4 Projection‑Stable Representation**
Representational patterns remain identifiable under upward and downward projection.

---

## **3. Sources of Representation Patterns**

Representation patterns arise from three independent structural mechanisms:

### **3.1 Recursive Symmetry**
Motifs repeat under recursive subdivision, preserving adjacency relationships.

### **3.2 Plateau Triplication**
The Chromatic 12 motif is represented independently in each γ‑plateau, producing three aligned copies.

### **3.3 Projection Compatibility**
Projection preserves motif identity, eliminating geometries with inconsistent representations.

---

## **4. Representation Classes**

Survivor geometries organize their internal structure into consistent representation classes.
These classes are structural, not interpretive.

### **4.1 Base Motif Representations**
The Chromatic 12 forms the foundational representation class.

### **4.2 Plateau‑Indexed Representations**
Each base motif has three plateau‑indexed representations:

- A‑representation
- B‑representation
- C‑representation

These are structurally aligned but context‑specific.

### **4.3 Adjacency‑Cycle Representations**
Modes participate in adjacency cycles that form higher‑order representation patterns.

### **4.4 Projection‑Invariant Representations**
Some representation patterns remain unchanged under projection, forming the “backbone” of survivor structure.

---

## **5. Representation and the 36‑Mode Structure**

Representation patterns directly support the 36‑mode structure:

### **5.1 Triplicated Motif Representation**
Each plateau represents the Chromatic 12 independently.

### **5.2 Cross‑Plateau Alignment**
Representations corresponding to the same base motif align across plateaus.

### **5.3 Mode‑Level Representation**
Each mode is a representational instance of a base motif within a specific plateau.

### **5.4 Recursive Representation Stability**
Representations remain stable under recursive subdivision, supporting long‑range coherence.

---

## **6. Representation Interactions (Structural Only)**

Representation interactions describe how representational patterns relate to one another.
These interactions are purely geometric.

### **6.1 Shared Representation Cycles**
Some motifs share representational cycles, forming composite structures.

### **6.2 Disjoint Representations**
Other motifs remain representationally disjoint, sharing no adjacency cycles.

### **6.3 Plateau‑Aligned Interactions**
Representation interactions in one plateau have structurally corresponding interactions in the other two.

### **6.4 Projection‑Preserved Interactions**
Interactions remain identifiable under projection, reinforcing representational stability.

---

## **7. Scope**

This document describes **only** the internal representation patterns of survivor geometries.
It does **not**:

- assign physical meaning to representations
- map representations to particles or fields
- invoke group theory or gauge symmetries
- define physical constants
- claim universality

Representation patterns are structural features of survivor geometries within the RCF sieve.
