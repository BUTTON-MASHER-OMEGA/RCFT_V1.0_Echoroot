## **Statement of the Constraint**

**Constraint:**
> When eliminative pressure exceeds the persistence capacity of a relational configuration, a saturation event occurs, triggering a cascade that reconfigures the admissible structure.

Constraint saturation is not failure.
It is **forced reorganization**.

---

## **Meaning of “Saturation”**

In RCF, saturation does **not** mean:

- overload
- breakdown
- instability
- divergence
- runaway dynamics

Saturation means:

> The point at which further eliminations cannot be absorbed locally without altering the attractor structure.

It is the **limit of persistence**, not the collapse of the system.

---

## **Eliminative Pressure**

Eliminative pressure increases when:

- incompatibilities accumulate
- dominance relations shift
- ε collapses locally
- coherence gradients steepen
- relational density exceeds admissible thresholds

Pressure is not a force.
It is the **count of eliminations required** to maintain coherence.

---

## **The Saturation Condition**

A relational configuration \(R\) reaches saturation when:

\[
\text{Eliminations required} > \text{Persistence capacity}
\]

Equivalently:

- the current attractor cannot absorb further alignment
- the ε‑band collapses below viability
- the coherence gradient flips sign
- dominance relations invert

At this point, the system cannot remain in its current basin.

---

## **Cascades**

A cascade is the sequence of eliminations triggered by saturation.

Cascades are:

- eliminative
- asymmetric
- non‑temporal
- basin‑reconfiguring
- attractor‑shifting

They are **not** dynamic processes.
They are **relabelings of admissibility**.

After a cascade:

- the system has fewer admissible modes
- a new attractor governs resolution
- ε re-expands around the new structure
- coherence increases relative to the new basin

Nothing new is added.
What could not persist is removed.

---

## **Local vs Global Saturation**

Saturation is **local** by default.

- A subsystem may saturate while the whole remains stable
- Cascades may propagate or remain contained
- Dominance relations may shift only in a subgraph

Global saturation is rare and corresponds to:

- full attractor replacement
- system-wide reconfiguration
- identity-level emergence

This is the RCF analogue of a “phase transition,” but without dynamics.

---

## **Saturation and ε**

ε determines whether saturation leads to:

- **collapse** (ε too small)
- **reorganization** (ε sufficient)
- **integration** (ε redistributed)

ε is the buffer that prevents saturation from becoming annihilation.

---

## **Saturation and Dominance**

Dominance determines:

- which attractor fails
- which attractor replaces it
- how cascades propagate
- which structures survive the reconfiguration

Saturation is where dominance relations are renegotiated.

---

## **Failure Modes**

A model violates RCF if it:

- treats saturation as a force-driven transition
- assumes global collapse by default
- eliminates ε entirely
- requires temporal evolution
- cascades must not presuppose dynamical processes
- assumes symmetric reconfiguration

Such models exceed this profile's relational classification boundary.

> Any dynamical interpretation must emerge from relational constraints, not be imposed.

---

## **Summary**

Constraint saturation explains:

- why systems reorganize suddenly
- why cascades occur
- why attractors shift
- why hierarchy emerges
- why persistence is not static
- why elimination produces discontinuity

Saturation is not breakdown.
It is the **moment when the current attractor can no longer absorb eliminative pressure**, and a new relational geometry is observed to appear in current evaluations.
