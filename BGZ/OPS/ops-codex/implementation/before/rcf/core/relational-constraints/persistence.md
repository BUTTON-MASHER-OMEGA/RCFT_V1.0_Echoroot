## Statement of the Constraint

**Constraint:**

> Only structures that persist under increasing relational constraint are admissible.

Persistence is not assumed.
Persistence is *tested*.

RCF does not posit what exists; it filters what survives.

---

## Meaning of “Persistence”

In RCF, persistence does **not** mean:

- permanence
- immutability
- equilibrium
- conservation
- longevity in time

Persistence means:

> The ability of a relational structure to maintain coherence as constraints tighten, relations change, or degrees of freedom are eliminated.

A structure may evolve, shed dimensions, or reconfigure and still be persistent — provided it does not dissolve entirely.

---

## Meaning of “Constraint”

A constraint is any condition that reduces admissible relational configurations, including but not limited to:

- overlap requirements
- incompatibility elimination
- degeneracy pressure
- boundary conditions
- resource limits
- coupling asymmetries
- environmental dominance

Constraints are **not** external forces; they are relational filters.

---

## Elimination, Not Construction

Persistence is evaluated through **elimination**, not addition.

RCF does not ask:

> “What must be added to make this structure exist?”

It asks:

> “What remains when incompatible relations are removed?”

If nothing remains, the structure is rejected.

---

## Domain Independence

This constraint applies equally to:

- physical structures
- cognitive patterns
- mathematical formalisms
- organizational systems
- social dynamics
- theoretical frameworks (including RCF itself)

Persistence is substrate-agnostic.

---

## Failure Modes

A structure fails the persistence constraint if:

- it requires continual fine-tuning to remain coherent
- it collapses when constraints increase
- it depends on privileged reference frames
- it dissolves under minor relational perturbation
- it cannot survive dimensional reduction

Such failures are eliminative, not correctable.

---

## Relation to Stability

Persistence is **not** the same as stability.

- Stability describes behavior *within* a configuration
- Persistence describes survival *across* configurations

A highly stable structure may still fail to persist if it cannot adapt to constraint changes.

---

## Relation to Time

Persistence is evaluated **without presupposing time**.

A structure may be indexed temporally in downstream models, but this shard does not assume:

- temporal evolution
- causal ordering
- arrow of time

Persistence is defined relationally, not temporally.

---

## Asymmetry and Dominance

Persistence is often asymmetric.

When multiple structures interact:

- the structure with higher memory mass or lower ε typically persists
- incompatible modes of the weaker structure are eliminated
- alignment occurs without requiring mutual collapse

Persistence does not require symmetry.

---

## Self-Applicability

This constraint applies to RCF itself.

If RCF fails to persist under its own constraints, it must be eliminated.

No exemption exists.

---

## Summary

Persistence under constraint is the **primary filter** of RCF.

Nothing is granted existence.
Nothing is preserved by fiat.
Nothing is protected from elimination.

Only what survives remains.
