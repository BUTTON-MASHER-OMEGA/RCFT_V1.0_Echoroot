## **Statement of the Constraint**

**Constraint:**
> An attractor is a relational configuration that remains invariant under constraint filtering and toward which nearby admissible configurations are ordered by the coherence gradient.

Attractors are not destinations.
They are **fixed points of elimination**.

---

## **What an Attractor Is (in RCF)**

In RCF, an attractor is:

> A configuration that minimizes eliminative cost and maximizes persistence under constraint.

An attractor:

- survives repeated constraint application
- aligns interacting structures asymmetrically
- narrows ε locally without annihilating it
- defines the basin of admissible relational modes

Attractors are **relational**, not intrinsic.

---

## **What an Attractor Is Not**

An attractor is **not**:

- a force
- a goal
- a stable equilibrium
- a preferred state
- a causal endpoint
- a temporal future

Attractors do not pull.
They simply **remain** when alternatives are eliminated.

---

## **Attractors and the Coherence Gradient**

The coherence gradient orders configurations by:

- eliminative cost
- persistence density
- compatibility with dominant structures

An attractor is a point where:

\[
\nabla_C R^* = 0
\]

Meaning:

- no further eliminations reduce incompatibility
- no alternative configuration is more coherent
- the structure is locally persistence‑favored in current evaluations.

The gradient does not move systems.
It orders them.

---

## **Attractor Basins**

A basin is the set of configurations that:

- share eliminative pathways
- converge under constraint
- align to the same dominant relational geometry

Basins are not spatial.
They are **equivalence classes of eliminative outcomes**.

Two configurations are in the same basin if:

- they eliminate to the same persistent structure
- they differ only by ε‑admissible modes
- they share a dominant attractor

---

## **Attractors and Dominance**

Dominance determines which attractor governs an interaction.

- The dominant structure defines the basin
- Subdominant structures align to it
- Incompatible modes are eliminated asymmetrically

Dominance selects the attractor.
The attractor shapes the resolution.

---

## **Attractors and ε**

ε is required for attractors to exist.

- If ε = 0, no alternatives exist → no basin
- If ε > 0, multiple admissible modes exist → basin structure emerges

Attractors are **ε‑filtered persistence minima**.

---

## **Attractors and Emergence**

Emergence is the appearance of attractor structure after:

- incompatible modes are eliminated
- coherence increases
- ε collapses locally
- the basin resolves

Nothing is added.
The attractor is what remains.

---

## **Contextuality**

Attractors are not universal.

- A structure may be an attractor in one context
- And unstable in another
- Basins shift as constraints shift
- Dominance relations reconfigure attractor geometry

There is no global attractor landscape.

---

## **Failure Modes**

A model violates RCF if it:

- treats attractors as forces
- assumes global attractor maxima
- requires temporal evolution
- collapses ε globally
- assumes symmetric convergence
- treats attractors as intrinsic entities

Such models reintroduce dynamics or metaphysics.

---

## **Summary**

Attractors are used to explain, within the present formalism:

- why resolution has direction
- why alignment stabilizes
- why hierarchy emerges
- why structure persists
- why elimination produces order

An attractor is not a destination.
It is the **relational configuration that survives everything else**.
