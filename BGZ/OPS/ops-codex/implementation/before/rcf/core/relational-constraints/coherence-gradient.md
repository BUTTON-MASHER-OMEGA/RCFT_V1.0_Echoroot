---

## **Statement of the Constraint**

**Constraint:**
> Interacting relational structures follow gradients of increasing coherence, moving toward configurations that minimize incompatibility under constraint.

The coherence gradient is not a force.
It is an ordering relation induced by constraint.

---

## **Meaning of “Coherence”**

In RCF, coherence does **not** mean:

- harmony
- agreement
- stability
- resonance
- alignment by intention

Coherence means:

> The degree to which a relational configuration survives constraint without eliminating its own internal structure.

Higher coherence = fewer eliminations required.
Lower coherence = more eliminations required.

---

## **Gradient, Not Dynamics**

RCF does **not** posit:

- motion
- evolution
- time
- trajectories
- causal flow

The coherence gradient is:

> A partial ordering over relational configurations based on relative eliminative cost.

A configuration \(R_1\) precedes \(R_2\) if:

- \(R_2\) requires fewer eliminations
- \(R_2\) retains more persistent structure
- \(R_2\) aligns better with dominant attractors

This ordering is **directional** but not temporal.

---

## **Attractor Basins**

A relational attractor is a configuration that:

- minimizes eliminative cost
- maximizes persistence
- stabilizes under repeated constraint
- narrows ε without annihilating it

Formally:

> An attractor is a fixed point of the coherence gradient.

Structures are observed, in current evaluations, to align with attractors as alternatives are eliminated.

---

## **Gradient and Dominance**

Dominance determines **which** attractor shapes the gradient.
The coherence gradient determines **how** alignment proceeds.

- Dominance sets the basin
- Coherence sets the slope
- ε sets the width of admissible paths

Together they define asymmetric resolution.

---

## **Gradient and ε**

ε is required for gradients to exist.

- If ε = 0, no alternatives exist → no gradient
- If ε > 0, multiple admissible configurations exist → gradient ordering emerges

The coherence gradient is the **filter** that selects among ε‑admissible modes.

---

## **Gradient and Emergence**

Emergence is the endpoint of gradient traversal.

- Eliminations prune incompatible modes
- Coherence increases
- Attractor is approached
- Residual structure appears as “emergent”

Nothing is added.
The gradient simply removes what cannot persist.

---

## **Relative, Not Absolute**

Coherence gradients are contextual:

- A structure may be high‑coherence in one interaction
- And low‑coherence in another
- Attractors shift with relational context
- Gradients reconfigure as constraints change

There is no universal coherence metric.

---

## **Failure Modes**

A model violates RCF if it:

- treats the gradient as a force
- assumes global coherence maxima
- requires temporal evolution
- collapses ε to zero
- assumes symmetric resolution
- treats attractors as intrinsic entities

Such models reintroduce dynamics by another name.

---

## **Summary**

The coherence gradient explains:

- why alignment has direction
- why attractors emerge
- why resolution is asymmetric
- why structure appears stable
- why elimination produces hierarchy

It is not motion.
It is not time.
It is the **ordering induced by constraint**.
