## Statement of the Constraint

**Constraint:**

> When interacting structures differ in relational inertia, alignment occurs toward the dominant attractor.

Dominance is not imposed.
Dominance emerges from persistence asymmetry.

---

## Meaning of “Dominance”

In RCF, dominance does **not** mean:

- authority
- agency
- control
- intention
- causation
- hierarchy by decree

Dominance means:

> The relative capacity of a structure to resist relational reconfiguration under interaction.

A dominant structure:

- sheds fewer modes under constraint
- retains coherence across interactions
- defines admissible relations for weaker structures

Dominance is relational and contextual, not absolute.

---

## Relational Inertia

Dominance arises from **relational inertia**, which may include:

- memory mass
- internal constraint density
- spectral compression
- historical accumulation
- reduced ε-band width

Higher inertia ⇒ lower susceptibility to reconfiguration.

---

## Alignment, Not Collapse

When structures interact, RCF does not posit collapse.

Instead:

- incompatible relational modes are eliminated
- surviving modes align to the dominant attractor
- resolution occurs asymmetrically

Alignment is eliminative, not projective.

---

## Asymmetric Resolution

Given two interacting systems A and B:

- If A occupies the dominant attractor in the interaction context:
  - B’s incompatible modes are eliminated
  - B aligns to A’s attractor
  - A remains largely unchanged

This does **not** imply:

- global state collapse
- observer privilege
- mutual entanglement
- symmetric resolution

Asymmetry is expected.

---

## Relative Superposition

Prior to interaction:

- A may occupy a dominant mode
- B may retain multiple admissible relational modes relative to A

This is **relative superposition**, not entanglement.

Superposition here denotes unresolved relational compatibility, not quantum state structure.

---

## Cascades and Integration

If interaction persists:

- alignment reduces ε locally
- cascades may occur
- A and B may be treated as a new effective system downstream

The resulting system inherits:

- reduced option space
- altered dominance relations
- new persistence constraints

Integration is contingent, not guaranteed.

---

## Non-Privileged Roles

Dominance does not imply observer status.

- A and B are interchangeable under relabeling
- dominance depends on context, not identity
- any system may be dominant in one interaction and subordinate in another

There is no privileged system class.

---

## Failure Modes

A dominance model fails RCF constraints if it:

- assumes symmetric resolution by default
- requires observer intervention
- postulates universal collapse
- assigns intrinsic dominance
- eliminates ε globally

Such models over-specify resolution.

---

## Relation to Persistence

Dominance is derivative of persistence.

- Persistence determines survivability
- Dominance determines alignment direction
- Alignment determines local resolution

Dominance cannot override persistence.

---

## Summary

Dominance is used to explain, within the present formalism **why alignment occurs without observers**,
**why resolution is asymmetric**,
and **why interaction does not require collapse**.

Structures do not negotiate.
They align where resistance is lowest.

That is dominance.
