## **Purpose**

Define **dominance**, **alignment**, and **perturbative influence** as structural relations between interacting relational states under constraint.

This module introduces no ontology, mechanism, or domain assumptions.

---

## **1. Dominant Attractors**

A **dominant attractor** is a relational configuration that:

- persists under increased constraint
- exhibits higher effective inertia or memory mass
- defines admissibility conditions for interaction
- resists reconfiguration relative to coupled systems

Dominance is **contextual**, not intrinsic.
It is defined only relative to a specific interaction boundary.

---

## **2. Alignment**

**Alignment** is the elimination of incompatible relational modes during interaction with a dominant attractor.

Properties:

- alignment is constraint resolution
- alignment introduces no new structure
- alignment does not require observation
- alignment is directional only because dominance is directional

Formally:

If system \(A\) occupies a dominant attractor in the interaction context, then system \(B\) resolves its admissible modes relative to the constraints defined by \(A\).

Alignment is eliminative, not selective.

---

## **3. Relative Admissibility**

Before interaction, a non‑dominant system may maintain multiple admissible relational modes relative to a dominant system.

This reflects:

- unresolved relational alternatives
- ε‑supported option space
- absence of constraint‑driven resolution

The dominant system does not share this multiplicity.

This asymmetry is structural.

---

## **4. Perturbative Influence**

A non‑dominant system may exert **perturbative influence** on a dominant system.

Perturbation means:

- introducing additional constraint pressure
- modifying interface admissibility
- biasing resolution pathways

Perturbation does **not** imply:

- inversion of dominance
- symmetric alignment
- alteration of core invariants

Perturbation occurs within ε and does not redefine attractor structure.

---

## **5. Cascade Resolution**

During interaction:

1. constraint pressure increases
2. ε narrows locally
3. incompatible modes are eliminated
4. one admissible configuration survives
5. a new effective relational state forms

This process is a **cascade**.
It is eliminative and locally irreversible.

---

## **6. Non‑Privileged Observers**

Dominance and alignment:

- do not depend on observers
- do not require measurement
- do not introduce epistemic asymmetry

Observer/system labels are interchangeable under relabeling.

Within this formalism, resolution is classified from the declared constraint structure alone.

---

## **7. Structural Invariant**

> Dominance defines admissibility.
> Alignment eliminates incompatible modes.
> Perturbation biases resolution without altering dominance.

This invariant applies uniformly across all relational systems expressible in RCD.
