## **Purpose**

Define **cascade** as the constraint‑driven reduction of admissible relational modes.

Cascade introduces no dynamics, ontology, or mechanism.
It is a structural outcome of constraint application.

---

## **1. Definition**

A **cascade** is the irreversible reduction of admissible relational modes caused by accumulated constraint pressure.

Formally:

\[
R \xrightarrow{\{\mathcal{C}_i\}} R_{\text{resolved}}
\]

Where:

- \(R\) is a relational state with multiple admissible modes
- \(\{\mathcal{C}_i\}\) is a set of constraint operators
- \(R_{\text{resolved}}\) is the surviving admissible configuration

Cascade is eliminative, not generative.

---

## **2. Accumulation, Not Trigger**

Cascades do not require initiating events.

They arise from:

- accumulated constraint
- narrowing of ε
- saturation of incompatibility

A cascade occurs when **no alternative modes remain admissible**.

---

## **3. Threshold Condition**

A cascade occurs when:

> The admissible relational set reduces from multiple modes to a single surviving configuration under existing constraints.

This threshold is local to the interaction context.

---

## **4. Directionality and Irreversibility**

Cascades are:

- directional (admissibility decreases monotonically)
- locally irreversible
- dependent on constraint history

Reversal requires explicit constraint relaxation.

---

## **5. Dominance‑Guided Resolution**

In interacting systems \(A\) and \(B\):

- the dominant attractor defines admissibility
- incompatible modes of the non‑dominant system are eliminated
- alignment emerges through elimination

Dominance is contextual and does not imply symmetry.

---

## **6. ε‑Band Behavior**

During cascade:

- ε narrows within the interaction boundary
- ε does not vanish globally
- residual ε persists outside the resolved region

This preserves non‑zero possibility at scale.

---

## **7. Non‑Collapse Status**

Cascade is **not**:

- collapse
- measurement
- selection
- observer‑dependent

It is constraint resolution under admissibility.

---

## **8. Domain‑Neutral Applicability**

Cascade applies uniformly to any system expressible in RCD.

Differences arise from constraint structure, not cascade mechanics.

---

## **9. Ordering, Not Duration**

Cascade defines an **ordering relation**, not a temporal process.

Sequence labels order cascade records only; any temporal indexing belongs to the downstream Time module.

---

## **10. Self‑Applicability**

Cascade applies to RCD itself.

Any RCD construct requiring cascade exceptions is invalid.
