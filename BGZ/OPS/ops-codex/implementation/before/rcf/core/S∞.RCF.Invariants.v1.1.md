## **0. Scope and Intent**

This document defines the **core invariants** of RCF.

These invariants:

- are **pre‑theoretical**
- do **not** depend on any physical, cognitive, or philosophical substrate
- must be preserved by any RCF‑compatible module
- cannot be modified, expanded, or interpreted as mechanisms

RCF is defined by these constraints.

---

## **1. Persistence Under Constraint**

**Invariant:**
> Structures that persist under increasing relational constraint are meaningful; those that do not are eliminated.

RCF:

- does not construct entities
- filters survivability
- defines meaning operationally as *persistence*

---

## **2. Emergence via Elimination**

**Invariant:**
> Structure emerges through the elimination of unstable relations, not through additive construction.

RCF does not introduce primitives.
It applies constraint and retains only what survives.

---

## **3. Relational Primacy**

**Invariant:**
> No entity possesses intrinsic meaning outside its relations.

Identity is relational.
No subject/object distinction is fundamental.

---

## **4. Spectral Dominance**

**Invariant:**
> Persistent structure is encoded spectrally, not configurationally.

States are transient.
Spectra persist.

---

## **5. Constraint Over Dynamics**

**Invariant:**
> RCF specifies admissibility conditions, not equations of motion.

Change is treated as constraint variation, not as fundamental dynamics.

---

## **6. Minimal Free Parameters**

**Invariant:**
> Valid RCF‑compatible structures minimize tunable constants.

Structural ratios must survive parameter elimination.

---

## **7. Non‑Privileged Observers**

**Invariant:**
> Observation does not create reality; it constrains relational resolution.

No observer is ontologically special.
Observer/system roles are interchangeable under relabeling.

---

## **8. Self‑Applicability**

**Invariant:**
> The framework must be applicable to itself without contradiction.

RCF concepts must survive their own constraints.

---

## **9. ε‑Allowance**

**Invariant:**
> No complex system collapses to a single mode without residual alternatives.

Dominant modes provide stability.
ε‑bands preserve adaptability.
Possibility remains nonzero.

---

## **10. Invariant Closure**

These invariants are:

- jointly sufficient
- mutually reinforcing
- minimal

Any extension or module must:

1. preserve all invariants
2. introduce no contradiction
3. remain eliminable without damaging the core

---

## **Closing Note**

RCF is defined not by what it explains,
but by **what it refuses to allow**.
