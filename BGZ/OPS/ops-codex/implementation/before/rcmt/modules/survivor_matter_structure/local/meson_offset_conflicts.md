## Overview
This document explores a candidate RCMT interpretation in which meson‑like behavior may be modeled as **two‑phase offset conflicts** within a shared survivor geometry. The goal is to describe the identity structure of these configurations without pre‑empting the downstream geometry‑behavior module, which will address collapse dynamics, mismatch gradients, and reinjection cycles.

Meson‑like structures are treated here as **transient offset regimes**, not as fundamental entities.

## Two-Phase Offset Structure (Interpretive)
A meson‑like configuration is modeled as a survivor geometry caught between two admissible offset orientations:

- A ↔ B
- B ↔ C
- or A ↔ C

These two‑phase regimes lack the stabilizing influence of a full three‑phase cycle (A → B → C → A). As a result, they may exhibit:

- internal tension between offset orientations,
- oscillatory behavior between two phase positions,
- and sensitivity to local mismatch conditions.

This interpretation is structural rather than ontological.

## Comparison with Multi-Phase (Baryon-Like) Cycles
In contrast to baryon‑like survivors, which may stabilize through a full A/B/C cycle, two‑phase configurations do not have access to the same stabilizing geometry. Without the third phase, the offset cycle may be:

- incomplete,
- less coherent,
- and more susceptible to changes in imported LCO admissibility records.

This provides a structural basis for treating meson‑like configurations as **short‑lived** or **transient** within the refinement process.

## LCO Evaluation (Interpretive)
Under this interpretation, imported LCO records may classify two-phase offset conflicts as:

- admissible for short intervals,
- but not minimal or stable over extended evaluation windows.

This is not a claim about physical decay processes.
It is a structural description of how offset conflicts may behave under RCMT’s refinement rules.

## Mismatch Considerations (Boundary Only)
Two‑phase offset conflicts may exhibit higher mismatch amplitude than near‑zero or shallow offsets, but lower mismatch than deep multi‑phase cycles. This file does not describe:

- mismatch propagation,
- curvature‑like gradients,
- or collapse/reinjection behavior.

Those topics belong to the Geometry‑Behavior Module.

## Interpretive Role
Meson‑like offset conflicts serve as a useful interpretive category for:

- transient survivors,
- incomplete phase cycles,
- and offset regimes that do not stabilize into electron‑like, neutrino‑like, or baryon‑like structures.

This category is provisional and intended to support downstream refinement.

## Summary
Meson‑like behavior is modeled here as a **two‑phase offset conflict** within a shared survivor geometry. These configurations are structurally distinct from both shallow offsets and deep multi‑phase cycles, and may provide a compact interpretive framework for short‑lived or transitional survivor regimes. This interpretation remains open to revision as Geometry-Behavior evaluations develop.
