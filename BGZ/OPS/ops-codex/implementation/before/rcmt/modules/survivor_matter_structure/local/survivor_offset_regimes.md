## Overview
This document explores a candidate RCMT interpretation in which fermion‑like behaviors may arise from **offset regimes** of a shared survivor geometry.
The goal is to provide a compact structural vocabulary for downstream reasoning without asserting a canonical ontology.

The survivor geometry used here is the **120‑cell**, treated as a candidate model for persistent configurations under LCO evaluation.

## Offset Depth (Interpretive)
Offset depth is used as a structural parameter describing how far a survivor deviates from its least‑friction orientation.

This interpretation tests offset depth as a correlate of:

- mismatch amplitude,
- exhaust behavior,
- curvature‑like contribution,
- and admissibility across ordered LCO evaluations.

Offset depth is not treated as a physical field, but as a geometric descriptor within the RCMT refinement process.

## Four Offset Regimes (Interpretive Categories)

### 1. Electron‑like (Near‑Zero Offset)
A survivor configuration close to its least‑friction orientation.
These states exhibit:

- minimal mismatch,
- high stability,
- low exhaust.

This regime is used as a candidate model for electron‑like behavior.

### 2. Neutrino‑like (Shallow Offset)
A survivor with a small but persistent offset.
These states may exhibit:

- low mismatch amplitude,
- weak coupling to surrounding structures,
- minimal curvature‑like influence.

This regime is used as a candidate model for neutrino‑like behavior.

### 3. Baryon‑like (Deep Multi‑Phase Offset)
A survivor with a deep offset that cycles through multiple admissible orientations.
These states may exhibit:

- high mismatch amplitude,
- persistent phase‑cycle behavior,
- strong confinement‑like stability.

This regime is used as a candidate model for baryon‑like behavior.

### 4. Meson‑like (Two‑Phase Offset Conflict)
A survivor caught between two incompatible offset orientations.
These states may exhibit:

- unstable cycling,
- rapid changes in imported LCO admissibility records,
- collapse into simpler survivors.

This regime is used as a candidate model for meson‑like behavior.

## Notes
These categories are interpretive and intended to support downstream geometry‑behavior work.
They do not assert a physical ontology and remain open to refinement.
