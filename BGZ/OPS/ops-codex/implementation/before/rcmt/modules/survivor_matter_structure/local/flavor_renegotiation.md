## Overview
This document explores a candidate RCMT interpretation in which flavor‑like transitions may be modeled as **deep offset renegotiations** within a shared survivor geometry. The goal is to describe the identity structure of these transitions without asserting a physical mechanism or pre‑empting the downstream geometry‑behavior module, which will address mismatch redistribution, collapse behavior, and reinjection cycles.

Flavor‑like behavior is treated here as a **structural reconfiguration** of the survivor’s offset regime rather than as a change in fundamental identity.

## Deep Offset Regimes (Interpretive)
Within this interpretation, a survivor geometry may occupy multiple deep offset regimes, each corresponding to a distinct structural configuration. These regimes may differ in:

- offset depth,
- phase‑cycle tension,
- semantic alignment with SMG modes,
- and local mismatch distribution.

A shift between these regimes is treated as a **renegotiation** rather than a discrete transformation.

## Structural Interpretation of Flavor-Like Behavior
Flavor‑like transitions are modeled as the survivor temporarily entering a different deep offset regime. This may involve:

- a rebalancing of mismatch across the geometry,
- a shift in phase‑cycle timing or amplitude,
- or a reorientation toward a different semantic mode inherited from SMG.

These transitions are not treated as particle‑level events, but as **geometric reconfigurations** within the refinement process.

## LCO Evaluation (Interpretive)
Under this interpretation, imported LCO records may classify a deep offset renegotiation as:

- admissible, allowing the survivor to stabilize in a new regime, or
- inadmissible, prompting a return to the previous regime or a transition to a simpler survivor.

This file does not describe the dynamics of such transitions, which belong to the geometry‑behavior module.

## Relation to Phase-Cycle Structure
Deep offset renegotiation may interact with the A/B/C phase‑cycle structure in several ways:

- shifting the relative stability of phase positions,
- altering the timing of the cycle,
- or changing the mismatch profile that supports the cycle.

These interactions are treated as structural possibilities rather than fixed rules.

## Comparison with Two-Phase Offset Conflicts
Flavor‑like renegotiation differs from meson‑like offset conflicts in that:

- it involves transitions between deep offset regimes rather than instability within a single regime,
- it may lead to a new stable configuration rather than collapse,
- and it is not limited to two‑phase oscillation.

This distinction is interpretive and may be refined as downstream modules develop.

## Interpretive Role
Flavor‑like renegotiation provides a structural vocabulary for describing:

- transitions between deep offset regimes,
- shifts in semantic alignment,
- and reconfigurations of survivor geometry under LCO evaluation.

These interpretations are provisional and intended to support downstream refinement.
