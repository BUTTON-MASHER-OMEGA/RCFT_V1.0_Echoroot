# **Survivor Contract (SC‑1.0)**
*(RCMT → RCF‑compatible, RCP‑typed)*

This contract defines what counts as a **survivor** in RCMT.
Survivors are stable mismatch‑binding structures that persist across evaluations and expose interpretable structure to projection.

This contract ensures survivors remain **upstream objects**, not geometric or field‑like entities.

---

## **1. Purpose**

> **Define the canonical criteria for survivor formation, stability, and interface exposure.**

Survivors are the upstream origin of matter‑like behavior in RCMT.

**Type:** Purpose
**Owner:** RCMT survivor module

---

## **2. Ownership**

The survivor module owns:

- the definition of survivor stability
- the binding criteria for mismatch
- the upstream semantics of survivor persistence
- the interface for what survivors expose to projection

The survivor module does **not** own:

- geometric interpretation
- field‑like interpretation
- mass, charge, spin, or curvature
- projection smoothing

**Type:** Invariant

---

## **3. Survivor Definition**

A **survivor** is:

> **a stable, repeatable binding of mismatch that persists across adjacency evaluations and maintains internal coherence under LCO constraints.**

Survivors:

- bind mismatch
- resist dissolution
- maintain structural identity
- expose stable patterns to projection

Survivors are **not** geometric objects.

**Type:** Invariant (category definition)

---

## **4. Binding Criteria**

Mismatch is considered **bound** when:

- it forms a stable adjacency‑consistent pattern
- the pattern persists across evaluations
- the pattern resists free propagation
- the pattern maintains internal coherence under LCO

Binding is **observed**, not mechanistically explained.

**Type:** Observation

---

## **5. Allowed Inputs**

The survivor module consumes:

- locked mismatch
- structured mismatch
- adjacency relations
- LCO coherence scoring

**Type:** Dependency

---

## **6. Allowed Outputs**

The survivor module exposes:

- stable mismatch‑binding patterns
- survivor identity labels
- survivor adjacency signatures
- survivor‑level structural profiles
- spin‑compatible orientation patterns
- mode‑bearing internal structure

These outputs:

- are upstream structures,
- may be interpreted by projection as geometric,
- but are not geometric objects on their own.

**Type:** Interface Output

---

## **7. Survivor → Projection Interface**

Projection may interpret survivor outputs as:

- mass‑like behavior
- charge‑like behavior
- spin‑like behavior
- mode‑like structure
- geometric‑lane anchors

But these are **downstream interpretations**, not upstream facts.

**Type:** Downstream Interpretation

---

## **8. Prohibitions**

Survivor definitions must not:

- imply geometry
- imply fields
- imply forces
- imply continuous manifolds
- imply causal mechanisms
- redefine mismatch categories
- widen upstream invariants

**Type:** Prohibition

---

## **9. Scope Binding**

All survivor statements must be scoped:

- “within the present formalism”
- “as upstream survivor behavior”
- “prior to projection”
- “in the survivor module”

This prevents downstream leakage.

**Type:** Contract

---

## **10. Observations (Current Evaluations)**

> **In current evaluations, survivors appear as stable mismatch‑binding structures that maintain identity and expose consistent patterns to projection.**

This is observational, not canonical.

**Type:** Observation
