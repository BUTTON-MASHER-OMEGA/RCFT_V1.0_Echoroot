# Survivor–Mode Coupling Contract (SMC‑1.0)
*RCMT → Projection, RCP‑typed*

This contract defines how **modes** couple to **survivors** upstream, producing the structured patterns that projection interprets as **matter‑like entities** with **spin/charge‑like attributes** and **persistent identity**.

Upstream, this is just stable structure plus internal patterning.
Downstream, it looks like particles.

---

## 1. Purpose

**Purpose:**
Define the upstream structural relationship between survivors and modes, and the conditions under which projection may interpret their coupling as **matter‑like**.

**Type:** Purpose
**Owner:** Survivor–mode coupling module

---

## 2. Ownership

The survivor–mode coupling module owns:

- the definition of **matter‑compatible** survivor–mode patterns
- the relationship between survivor identity and mode structure
- the upstream semantics of “internal degrees of freedom”

It does **not** own:

- particle ontology
- mass, charge, spin as physics primitives
- interaction laws
- downstream dynamics

**Type:** Invariant

---

## 3. Matter‑compatible configuration

A configuration is **matter‑compatible** when:

> **a stable survivor carries a persistent mode pattern that maintains coherence and stability across propagation within a basin.**

Requirements:

- a **survivor** (stable mismatch‑binding structure)
- a **mode** defined on or within that survivor
- coherence above threshold
- stability under adjacency updates
- basin compatibility

This is a **structural condition**, not a particle definition.

**Type:** Invariant (category definition)

---

## 4. Upstream roles

In matter‑compatible configurations:

- **survivors** supply persistent identity and structural backbone
- **modes** supply internal patterning (spin/charge‑like structure)
- **coherence + stability** gate persistence
- **basins** determine which survivor–mode combinations are admissible

Upstream, this is **stable patterned structure**, not “matter.”

**Type:** Dependency

---

## 5. Allowed inputs

The survivor–mode coupling module consumes:

- survivor structures and identity labels
- mode structures and mode families
- coherence scores
- stability evaluations
- basin structure
- propagation traces (for persistence checks)

**Type:** Dependency

---

## 6. Allowed outputs

The survivor–mode coupling module exposes:

- matter‑compatible survivor–mode signatures
- survivor‑anchored mode patterns
- persistence‑qualified structural identities

These outputs:

- **are upstream structures**,
- **may be interpreted by projection as particles with spin/charge‑like attributes**,
- **but are not particles on their own.**

**Type:** Interface Output

---

## 7. Survivor–mode → projection interface

Projection may interpret survivor–mode patterns as:

- particle‑like entities
- mass‑like persistence
- spin‑like states
- charge‑like labels
- internal degrees of freedom

These are **downstream interpretations**, not upstream facts.

**Type:** Downstream Interpretation

---

## 8. Prohibitions

Survivor–mode coupling must not:

- define particle ontology
- define interaction laws
- introduce metric or manifold structure
- redefine survivors or modes
- widen upstream invariants

**Type:** Prohibition

---

## 9. Scope binding

All survivor–mode coupling statements must be scoped:

- “within the present formalism”
- “as upstream matter‑compatible structure”
- “prior to projection”
- “in the survivor–mode coupling module”

**Type:** Contract
