This document specifies how **measurement** is represented in Relational Coherence Mode Theory (RCMT).

It is downstream of the RCMT invariants, symmetry_chain, mode_space, and projection specifications.

Measurement here is a **structural pattern**:
it is modeled as **boundary‑constraint resolution**, not as a primitive physical process or ontological collapse.

---

## 1. Structural role of measurement

In RCMT, “measurement” refers to situations where:

- a local region is subject to **explicit boundary conditions**, and
- mode‑space structure must be evaluated under those constraints, and
- only configurations compatible with the boundary survive LCO evaluation.

Admissible models must represent measurement as **constraint resolution**, not as:

- an external intervention,
- a special dynamical rule,
- or a distinct ontological event.

---

## 2. Boundary‑constraint resolution

Measurement, entanglement, and decoherence are all modeled as instances of **boundary‑constraint resolution**:

- a boundary condition is imposed (experiment, apparatus, environment),
- mode‑space configurations are evaluated by the LCO,
- incompatible configurations fail to survive,
- compatible configurations define the resulting projected state.

Admissible models must:

- represent measurement using this structural pattern,
- avoid introducing non‑local collapse mechanisms,
- and remain compatible with adjacency‑local information flow.

---

## 3. Role of the LCO in measurement

The Local Coherence Operator (LCO):

- evaluates local mismatch under the imposed boundary,
- enforces coherence thresholds,
- and determines which configurations are admissible.

This is a **representational requirement**, not an agency claim.

Admissible models must:

- define measurement in a form that allows LCO evaluation,
- avoid treating the LCO as a physical agent,
- and avoid introducing global evolution laws.

---

## 4. Projection during measurement

During measurement, projection:

- maps admissible mode‑space configurations into the geometric lane,
- exposes observable-outcome records as representational shadows,
- and does not introduce new degrees of freedom.

Admissible models must:

- treat outcomes as projected configurations compatible with the boundary,
- avoid interpreting projection as ontological collapse,
- and maintain compatibility with the Mode‑Space Projection Invariant.

---

## 5. Decoherence and entanglement

Within RCMT:

- **Decoherence** is boundary‑constraint resolution where many environmental degrees of freedom participate, suppressing incompatible mode‑patterns.

- **Entanglement** is shared constraint structure across regions, such that boundary resolution in one region constrains admissible configurations in another.

Admissible models must:

- represent both as structural consequences of boundary‑constraint resolution,
- avoid non‑local signaling,
- and remain compatible with the Local Information Flow Invariant.

---

## 6. Probabilistic outcomes

RCMT represents probabilistic measurement outcomes using:

- unresolved mode‑structure in the symplectic lane,
- the geometry of projection,
- and the imposed boundary constraints.

Admissible models must:

- treat probabilities as induced measures on mode‑space,
- avoid treating randomness as fundamental,
- and remain compatible with the Randomness Representation Constraint.

---

## 7. No special measurement postulate

RCMT does not introduce a separate “measurement postulate.”

Admissible models must:

- treat measurement as a special case of boundary‑constraint resolution,
- use the same LCO and projection structures as in non‑measurement contexts,
- and avoid dual dynamics (e.g., unitary vs. collapse).

---

- **Randomness Representation Constraint**
