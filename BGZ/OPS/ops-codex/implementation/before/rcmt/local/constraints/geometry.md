This document defines the **geometric lane** in Relational Coherence Mode Theory (RCMT).

It is downstream of the RCMT invariants, mode‑space, projection, LCO, and cosmology specifications.

The geometric lane is a **representational domain** for:

- local Lorentz‑compatible structure,
- compact internal symmetry,
- and finite geometric configurations.

It is not an ontological spacetime manifold.

---

## 1. Structural role of the geometric lane

The geometric lane provides:

- a local representation of adjacency,
- a local representation of causal structure,
- and a local representation of internal symmetry.

Admissible models must:

- treat the geometric lane as a projection target,
- avoid treating it as a fundamental background,
- and avoid introducing global manifold structure.

---

## 2. Local Lorentz compatibility

Geometric representations must be **locally Lorentz‑compatible**:

- local neighborhoods admit an \(SO(3,1)\) structure,
- causal cones are locally well‑defined,
- and the speed \(c\) bounds adjacency‑update rates.

Admissible models must:

- enforce Lorentz compatibility locally,
- avoid global Lorentz frames,
- and maintain compatibility with adjacency‑local information flow.

---

## 3. Compact internal symmetry

Internal symmetry in the geometric lane must be:

- compact,
- finite‑dimensional,
- and locally representable.

Admissible models must:

- avoid non‑compact global internal groups,
- avoid infinite symmetry towers,
- and maintain compatibility with the Finite Structure Invariant.

---

## 4. Projection into the geometric lane

Projection maps mode‑space structure into the geometric lane.

Admissible models must:

- define projection so that geometric quantities are shadows of mode‑structure,
- avoid introducing geometric primitives not reducible to projection,
- and ensure that projection does not create new degrees of freedom.

The geometric lane is **downstream** of mode‑space.

---

## 5. No global manifold or global curvature

RCMT does not permit:

- a global spacetime manifold,
- global curvature groups,
- or global geometric evolution laws.

Within RCMT, downstream curvature descriptors are treated as:

- local,
- compatible with upstream mismatch and geometry-dynamics records,
- and representational.

Admissible models must:

- treat curvature as a local geometric shadow compatible with upstream LCO and geometry-dynamics records,
- avoid global curvature assignments,
- and maintain compatibility with the Local Information Flow Invariant.

---

## 6. Finite representational structure

Geometric representations must be:

- finite in local regions,
- adjacency‑compatible,
- and LCO‑evaluatable.

Admissible models must:

- avoid infinite local structures,
- avoid continuum assumptions as fundamental,
- and treat any continuum limit as an approximation.

---

## 7. Relationship to cosmology

At large scales, the geometric lane represents:

- coherence basins,
- mismatch geometry,
- and large‑scale adjacency patterns.

Admissible models must:

- maintain consistency between local geometric structure and cosmological behavior,
- avoid introducing separate cosmological geometry,
- and treat all geometry as projection‑derived.
