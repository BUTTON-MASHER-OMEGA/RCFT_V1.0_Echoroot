# RCMT Decoherence

This document defines **decoherence** as represented in Relational Coherence Mode Theory (RCMT).

It is downstream of the RCMT invariants, projection, measurement, and LCO specifications.

Decoherence is modeled as **environmental boundary‑constraint resolution**, not as a dynamical process, collapse mechanism, or ontological transition.

---

## 1. Structural role of decoherence

Within RCMT, a decoherence record is admissible when:

- a local region interacts with a large number of environmental constraints, and
- incompatible mode‑patterns are eliminated by LCO evaluation, and
- only stable projected configurations remain admissible.

Decoherence is a **structural filtering effect**, not a physical process.

---

## 2. Environmental boundary constraints

The environment is represented as a **distributed constraint structure** that:

- imposes many weak but cumulative constraints,
- suppresses incompatible mode‑patterns,
- and stabilizes a small set of admissible projections.

Admissible models must:

- represent environmental influence as boundary constraints,
- avoid treating the environment as a dynamical bath,
- and avoid introducing non‑local collapse behavior.

---

## 3. Mode‑pattern suppression

Under environmental constraints:

- unresolved mode‑structure is reduced,
- incompatible configurations fail LCO evaluation,
- and stable mode‑patterns dominate projection.

RCMT may interpret this filtered structure downstream as classical behavior.

Mode‑pattern suppression is representational, not ontological.

---

## 4. Pointer‑basis stability

Within RCMT, a **pointer-basis label** records:

- a set of projected configurations,
- that remain stable under repeated LCO evaluation,
- given the imposed environmental constraints.

Admissible models must:

- treat pointer‑basis stability as a structural consequence,
- avoid introducing preferred ontological bases,
- and maintain compatibility with adjacency‑local information flow.

---

## 5. Relationship to measurement

Measurement is a **high‑mismatch boundary condition**.
Decoherence is a **distributed low‑mismatch boundary condition**.

Both are instances of boundary‑constraint resolution:

- measurement eliminates all but one admissible configuration,
- decoherence eliminates many but not necessarily all incompatible configurations.

No special measurement dynamics are required.

---

## 6. Relationship to projection

Decoherence shapes projection by:

- reducing unresolved mode‑structure,
- stabilizing projected configurations,
- and suppressing interference between incompatible projections.

Admissible models must:

- treat decoherence as modifying admissible projections,
- avoid treating decoherence as a physical damping process,
- and maintain compatibility with the Mode‑Space Projection Invariant.

---

## 7. Emergence of classicality

RCMT represents classical behavior using:

- low unresolved mode‑structure,
- stable pointer‑basis configurations,
- and consistent projection across regions.

RCMT treats these records as sufficient model-local conditions for classical persistence.

No classical–quantum divide is required.

---

## 8. Compatibility with RCMT invariants

Decoherence representations must satisfy:

- **Local Coherence Operator Invariant**
- **Mode‑Space Projection Invariant**
- **Randomness Representation Constraint**
- **Local Information Flow Invariant**
- **Structural Identity Constraint**
