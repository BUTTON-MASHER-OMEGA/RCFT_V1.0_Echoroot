This document defines **structural identity** as represented in Relational Coherence Mode Theory (RCMT).
It is downstream of the RCMT invariants, projection, mode‑space, measurement, and LCO specifications.

Identity in RCMT is a **pattern‑persistence property**, not an intrinsic attribute or ontological label.

---

## 1. Structural role of identity

Within RCMT, an identity record contains:

- a stable relational configuration,
- that persists across locally admissible transitions,
- under repeated LCO evaluation,
- given the imposed boundary constraints.

Identity is not a substance, object, or intrinsic property.
It is a **coherence‑preserving pattern**.

---

## 2. Identity as pattern persistence

A configuration has identity when:

- its projected representation remains stable,
- its mode‑structure remains within admissible bounds,
- and its relational adjacency pattern is preserved.

Admissible models must:

- treat identity as persistence of structure,
- avoid introducing intrinsic identity markers,
- and avoid global identity assignments.

Identity is local and relational.

---

## 3. Identity loss

Identity is lost when:

- mismatch exceeds coherence thresholds,
- mode‑patterns become incompatible with local constraints,
- or projection yields a configuration outside the admissible region.

Identity loss is:

- structural,
- local,
- and representational.

It is not destruction, collapse, or annihilation.

---

## 4. Identity under projection

RCMT-local projection exposes the **visible representation** of an identity record.

Admissible models must:

- treat projected identity as a shadow of mode‑space structure,
- avoid interpreting projection as ontological persistence,
- and ensure identity is evaluated on projected configurations.

Identity is a projection‑dependent property.

---

## 5. Identity under decoherence

Decoherence stabilizes identity by:

- suppressing incompatible mode‑patterns,
- reducing unresolved structure,
- and enforcing pointer‑basis persistence.

Admissible models must:

- treat classical identity as decoherence‑stabilized pattern persistence,
- avoid introducing classical–quantum dual identity categories,
- and maintain compatibility with adjacency‑local information flow.

---

## 6. Identity in entangled systems

Entangled systems share constraint structure.
Identity in such systems is:

- distributed,
- relational,
- and boundary‑dependent.

Admissible models must:

- avoid assigning global identity to entangled components,
- treat identity as region‑specific pattern persistence,
- and avoid non‑local identity propagation.

---

## 7. Identity and measurement

Measurement imposes a high‑mismatch boundary.
Within RCMT, identity after measurement is represented by:

- the surviving projected configuration,
- that remains admissible under the imposed boundary.

RCMT does not treat measurement as creating identity; it records a stable pattern selected under the imposed boundary.

---

## 8. Identity and classical objects

Classical objects correspond to:

- highly stable mode‑patterns,
- low unresolved structure,
- and consistent projection across regions.

Their identity persists because:

- mismatch remains low,
- decoherence stabilizes patterns,
- and environmental constraints reinforce coherence.

Classical identity is emergent, not fundamental.
