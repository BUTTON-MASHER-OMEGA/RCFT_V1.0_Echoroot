## Purpose

This file defines the **charge pattern** in RCMT.

The charge pattern describes how persistent relational structures exhibit phase-like alignment behavior under a `U(1)`-type generator/action structure.

This is a pattern, not an ontology claim.

---

## PATTERN TYPE

- pattern class: identity-phase pattern
- symmetry source: `U(1)`-type structure
- primary structure: phase alignment
- identity status: alignment-dependent

---

## CONCEPTUAL DESCRIPTION

In this RCMT pattern, charge is modeled as a relational phase-alignment pattern.

Charge does not describe an intrinsic substance carried by an object.

It describes how a structure maintains or shifts phase compatibility under admissible transformation.

---

## STRUCTURAL ROLE

The charge pattern acts on:

- phase alignment
- identity basin compatibility
- boundary response
- projection grammar

It does not act on absolute space or hidden internal substance.

---

## GENERATOR ROLE

A charge generator defines an infinitesimal phase adjustment.

The generator does not preserve identity.

It defines a permitted direction in phase-alignment space.

---

## ACTION ROLE

A charge action is a finite phase transformation built from charge generators.

After the action, LCO evaluation determines whether:

- the structure remains aligned
- the structure relocks into a compatible phase basin
- alignment fails

---

## REPRESENTATION ROLE

A charge representation describes how a relational structure responds to admissible phase transformations.

It records phase response behavior under LCO evaluation.

---

## ADMISSIBILITY CONDITIONS

The charge pattern is admissible when:

1. the transformation is allowed by the symmetry chain
2. phase compatibility remains coherent
3. mismatch remains below threshold
4. boundary compatibility is preserved
5. LCO evaluation accepts the transformed structure

---

## FAILURE MODES

The charge pattern fails when:

- phase alignment exceeds admissible bounds
- boundary compatibility fails
- mismatch exceeds threshold
- the structure cannot maintain or recover alignment

---

## NON-ONTOLOGY CLAUSE

Charge is not treated as an intrinsic particle substance in this pattern.

It is treated as a downstream relational phase-alignment behavior.
